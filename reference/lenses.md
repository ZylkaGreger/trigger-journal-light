# Lenses for pattern recognition

A curated collection of **lenses** for interpretation. They help name patterns
during the weekly reflection (`/week`) and shape **one** gentle open question.

## How to use these lenses (read this first)

- Lenses are for **noticing**, not for fixing. There is nothing to repair.
- **Never diagnose**, never label, never reinforce self-judgment.
  Not "you have a pain-body" — rather "could it be that…".
- They are **offerings**, not truths. The person doesn't have to agree with any of them.
- Apply at most **one or two** lenses per reflection — not all at once.
  Choose the ones the week's material genuinely supports.
- The result always ends on a **question to sit with**, not on advice.

---

## The core lenses

### The voice in the head
The endlessly commenting voice in your head is not *you* — it is just a voice.
The **story field** is usually this voice talking.
*Pattern:* the same way of narrating shows up again ("the others…", "always me…").
*Open question:* Who is actually hearing this voice? Would you be the voice —
or the one who notices it?

### The witness behind it / "Who am I?" (Ramana Maharshi)
Behind thoughts and feelings is that which *perceives*. You are not the
turmoil; you are the awareness in which it appears — no identity that has to
be held.
*Pattern:* entries where the person has completely merged with a role.
*Open question:* Who is there when the role isn't held for a moment?

### Stored charge — old imprints  ⟵ backbone of the pattern recognition
Old experiences that were never allowed all the way through get stored as
blocked energy. A trigger is usually **an old imprint being touched** — not
the event itself. Different triggers across weeks are often **the same imprint
on different surfaces** — exactly this is the "suspected shared root trigger".
*Pattern:* different situations, same body sensation / same fear underneath.
*Open question:* What if these three situations aren't three things at all,
but one thing knocking from several sides?

### Letting through instead of pushing down / the heart closing
When a disturbance comes up, we tend to contract (to "close") or to act it
out. The invitation: notice, relax, **let it through** — don't suppress,
don't hold on. "Let it out."
*Pattern:* the **body field** shows tightness/tension; reflex-like reacting.
*Open question:* Where exactly did something close up — and what would it
take to loosen there, just a little?

## Related voices from the same direction

### The second arrow (Buddhism)  ⟵ for `meta_trigger`
The first arrow is the event. The second one we shoot ourselves: the
suffering *about* the suffering, the upset about the upset — the "mental movie".
*Pattern:* `meta_trigger` is often set.
*Open question:* What if only the first arrow remained?

### "Is that really true?" (Byron Katie — The Work)
A gentle inquiry into the believed thought inside the **story**: Is it true?
Can I absolutely know it's true? Who would I be without this thought? This is
the long form of **"Are you sure it's out there?"**.
*Note:* offer as an invitation, never work through it as an interrogation.

### Parts / roles (IFS-adjacent)  ⟵ for `identity`
"The little grumbler", "I'm only ever this" are **parts**, not the whole. A
part can be seen without becoming it.
*Pattern:* recurring identity tags.
*Open question:* If "the little grumbler" is only a part of you — who is
holding it?

### The pain-body (Eckhart Tolle)
Accumulated emotional pain that "feeds" on matching situations and
reactivates itself. Explains why a small thing sometimes sets off a big wave.
*Pattern:* intensity that doesn't match the occasion; repetition.
*Open question:* Is the now reacting here — or something much older?

### RAIN (Tara Brach) — as a tone, not a task
Recognize, Allow, Investigate (gently), Nurture. Less an exercise, more the
**attitude** the reflection should be written in: kind, not correcting.

### Identity as a magnet
Identities attract matching situations, and then we react automatically. A
recurring identity often explains a recurring trigger pattern.
*Open question:* Which identity was so important today that it had to be
defended?

### "Why do I think I need it?" (in the spirit of *Conversations with God*)
Under every needing sits the assumption of a lack — that something is missing
and that I am not whole without it. The question doesn't touch the wish
itself (wishes are fine), but the quiet sentence underneath: *without this,
I am not enough.*
*Pattern:* entries where something is needed in order to be okay —
recognition, agreement, harmony, being right, being seen, everyone being fine.
*Open question:* **Why do I think I need it?**
*Note:* offer as an invitation, never as an unmasking. "I don't know" is a
completely sufficient answer — the question may stay open.

### Conditioning / the not-self (Human Design) — optional
Human Design describes areas where we are especially open to taking in and
amplifying what isn't ours — and a "not-self" flavor that shows up when we
live from that conditioning (e.g. proving ourselves, chasing recognition,
forcing decisions before they are ripe). If the person works with their own
chart, this can be *one* additional anchor for noticing.
*Pattern:* recurring pressure to prove something, to be recognized, or to
decide *right now*; upset that feels borrowed rather than one's own.
*Open question:* Could it be that this urgency isn't yours — and what remains
if you don't have to prove anything right now?
*Note:* a lens for noticing, never a label. Never "you are X, therefore…" —
always "could it be that…". When in doubt, leave it out.

---

*These lenses are not a therapy model and not a diagnosis. They are ways of
looking that share one spirit: observe, let through, let go — and leave the
question open.*
